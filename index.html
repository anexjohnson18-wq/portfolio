FEA / STRUCTURAL

Industrial Trolley Analysis

Modelled and assembled in CATIA, exported as STEP to ANSYS, then evaluated through meshing, static structural and buckling studies. Reported maximum deformation: 2.46 mm; maximum von Mises stress: 128.58 MPa; factor of safety above 1.94.

CATIA · ANSYS · STATIC STRUCTURAL · BUCKLING
Open report
Analysis process
  1. Model and assemble the trolley in CATIA, then export its geometry in STEP format.
  2. Import the geometry into ANSYS, prepare the mesh and apply the specified loads and gravity.
  3. Run static structural and buckling studies, then review deformation, von Mises stress, safety factor and buckling performance.
SOLIDWORKS · SHEET METAL

Three-Tier Sheet-Metal Shoe Rack

Designed and manufactured a three-tier sheet-metal shoe rack in SolidWorks, with space for three pairs of shoes per row. The three front doors are linked: opening one door opens all three tiers together. Developed the flat patterns and CypCut laser-cutting layout, then completed bending, welding and powder coating.

SOLIDWORKS · SHEET METAL · LINKED-DOOR MECHANISM · CypCut · FABRICATION
Design-to-manufacture process
  1. Model the three-tier rack in SolidWorks, including sheet-metal parts and the linked-door arrangement that lets one door operate all three tiers.
  2. Develop and flatten the sheet-metal parts to create manufacturing-ready profiles.
  3. Arrange the flat patterns in CypCut and prepare the nesting layout for laser cutting.
  4. Laser-cut and bend the components, then weld them into the rack assembly.
  5. Apply powder coating to finish the manufactured rack.

SHEET METAL · BBQ GRILL

Vertical BBQ Charcoal Grill

Developed a vertical charcoal BBQ grill as a sheet-metal product, taking the slotted body and assembled structure through a complete CAD-to-manufacturing workflow.

SHEET METAL · CAD DEVELOPMENT · FLAT PATTERNS · CypCut · LASER CUTTING · FABRICATION
Full design-to-manufacture process
  1. Develop the vertical grill assembly and individual sheet-metal components in CAD, checking how the body and supporting features fit together.
  2. Create manufacturing-ready flat patterns for the formed parts, including the required slots, cut-outs and interlocking features.
  3. Export the profiles and arrange them in CypCut for efficient laser-cutting nesting.
  4. Laser-cut the components and deburr the edges; bend or form the parts to their designed profiles.
  5. Fit and join the components to assemble the grill body, welding where required by the design.
  6. Apply a suitable protective finish to the exterior, using a heat-resistant finish on surfaces exposed to high temperatures, then inspect the completed assembly.
